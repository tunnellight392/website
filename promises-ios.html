How it works

A second implementation, not a port

The Android build is Java over Android Views, with AlarmManager doing the scheduling. Rewriting that structure in Swift would have produced something that worked and felt foreign. This one is built the way SwiftUI wants: state in @AppStorage and observable stores, notifications through UserNotifications, navigation and layout left to the framework.

What the two share is the content — the same public-domain translations, the same topics, the same verses. Where the platforms differ, this build follows iOS: Browse arrives as a sheet, topics push onto their own screens, and the verse is moved through by swiping rather than by a Next button.

Eighteen topics, and the ones you keep

Verses are grouped under eighteen topics — Addiction & Freedom, Anxiety & Worry, Children, Comfort & Grief, Depression, Faith, Fear, Forgiveness, Gratitude, Guidance, Healing, Hope, Loneliness, Love, Peace, Salvation, Strength and Trust — each listed with the number of promises behind it. Tapping the heart keeps a verse, and Favourites sits at the top of Browse rather than behind a separate tab.

Any promise can be shared as a ready-made picture card carrying the verse text, rather than as a bare line of copied text.

Reading on iOS terms

The verse can be set in a serif, sans-serif or monospaced face at a text size you choose, previewed live on the screen that sets it. Dynamic Type is honoured throughout, so the app follows the system text size a reader has already set rather than asking them to configure it a second time. Appearance follows light, dark or the system.

Private by design

It works offline with no account and no ads, and collects no data at all — favourites and settings stay on the device. Feedback opens the reader's own Mail app rather than sending anything from the app itself.
