How it works

The verse, and a way past it

The Promise of the Day sits over a photograph with its reference and translation credited underneath. Next Verse moves through the collection; Browse opens the topics. The heart saves it, and the share button sends the text on.

Topics that open where they sit

Verses are grouped under topics — Addiction & Freedom, Anxiety & Worry, Children, Comfort & Grief, Depression and the rest — each tile carrying its own image and the number of verses it holds, so the list tells you how much is behind it before you tap. A topic expands in place rather than pushing you onto another screen. Anything you have saved gathers into a Favorites group pinned to the top of the same list.

Two translations, all the way down

Switching the Bible version changes the verse, the book name and the credit line together, so the Malayalam reading is a whole screen in Malayalam rather than an English frame with the verse swapped out. Both translations are public domain: the World English Bible, and സത്യവേദപുസ്തകം (Malayalam O.V.).

Made readable

An accessibility screen sets the verse text size and face — serif, sans-serif or monospace — and previews the result live above the controls that change it, so you are choosing by looking at the thing itself. Themes follow light, dark or the system.

A notification you set, that survives a reboot

The daily verse arrives at a time you choose. Scheduling goes through AlarmManager and is re-registered after a restart, so a reboot does not quietly end the habit.

Suggesting a verse

A feedback screen inside the app takes bug reports, feature requests and suggestions for verses to add, without sending anyone to an email client.

A static page — no build step, served straight from the repository.
